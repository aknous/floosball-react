The game gets a page

Out of the modal and onto a route. A bar across the top returns to the board and steps through the sixteen games in the board’s own interest order, so next always means the next most interesting game. Left column is the game — scoreboard, line score, field, win probability, replay, and the full play-by-play, which keeps the modal’s accents — amber big plays with their win-probability swing, cyan clutch and red choke moments naming who rose or buckled, orange momentum shifts — and where expanding a play opens the same two-column insights panel the sim already exposes — situation and stratagem on the left, composure and execution on the right. Right column belongs to the fans: a watching count, the team page’s dropdown composer — grouped into lines about this play and lines you can post any time — and a permanent feed. A post about a play quotes that play above itself, and the players are in the feed too — in-character reactions to a play they were part of, and sideline cutaways between drives, each tinted by personality tier. One toggle drives the whole lower panel, the way the modal already does it — Plays, Box Score, Player Stats — with the play filters riding on the same bar so nothing competes with the live game. The mock is a standard four-quarter game; the line score, clock and win-probability axis are section-based in the source, so innings, frames, play-limit and chess-clock games swap columns and labels rather than breaking.
