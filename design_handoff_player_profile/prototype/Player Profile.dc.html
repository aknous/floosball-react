TURN 1

Who he is, then how good, then the numbers

The clutter today comes from three columns competing at the same weight and attributes hiding in a tab. Both directions use the shape you picked — a fixed identity rail on the left that never scrolls away, one content column on the right — and cut what you didn’t keep: no mental-profile panel, no percentile context, no game log. What stays: jersey and team, overall with offense and defense split, attribute bars, trophy case, recent moments, and the progression chart. Character — mood, awakened status, hometown, favourite and the quote — sits in one panel directly under the identity panel, with the trophy case and recent moments beneath it, so who he is reads before how good he is and the stats start higher. The awakened row is conditional: a player who hasn’t awakened shows no mention of it at all. Career tables use the stats page’s shell so a row reads the same everywhere. 1a and 1b differ in the hero, in how the career numbers are presented, and in density.

1a Standing Big jersey, ratings band, one career table with a side toggle.
